Insights into the 3D structure and behaviour of Icelandic crystal mushes from gabbroic nodules

Rahul Subbaramana, Margaret E. Hartleya, Jonathan Fellowesa, Margherita Polaccia, Barbara Bonechia, Lucia Pappalardob, Gianmarco Buonob and David A. Neavea
aDepartment of Earth and Environmental Sciences, University of Manchester, Oxford Road, Manchester, M13 9PL, UK
bIstituto Nazionale di Geofisica e Vulcanologia, Osservatorio Vesuviano, Sezione di Napoli, Via Diocleziano, 328, Napoli, 80125, NA, Italy
Available online 28 December 2025, Published: 15 February 2026.
Editor: Dr C. M. Petrone
Get content on ScienceDirect (DOI: 10.1016/j.epsl.2025.119791) ↗

Highlights
  • Gabbroic nodules sample plagioclase rich roof cumulates in upper crustal mushes.
  • Recycled plagioclase cores and zoning show non steady growth in homogeneous melts.
  • Volatile saturation and high vesicularity suggest free CO2 rich vapour.
  • Strong framework and connected bubble networks enable nodule preservation.
  • Loss of connectivity drives disaggregation into plagioclase rich crystal cargoes.
Abstract
Crystal mushes, porous yet cohesive frameworks of crystals interspersed with interstitial melts, form the plumbing systems of most active volcanoes. In Iceland, magmatic plumbing systems are inferred to be constructed from vertically stacked mushy magma reservoirs separated by subsolidus rock. Gabbroic nodules from Gígöldur in central Iceland provide a rare window into the structure, evolution and degassing behaviour of upper crustal (6 to 10 km) mushy magma reservoirs. These plagioclase rich nodules preserve recycled high An plagioclase cores, rim overgrowths and interstitial crystallisation of mafic phases, reflecting in situ modification within the final storage reservoir and roof cumulate formation via density driven segregation. Oscillatory zoning and variable rim patterns indicate that crystals experienced non steady state growth and were incorporated into clusters at different times. Compositional similarities between carrier and interstitial melts demonstrate that the final stages of mush evolution occurred in relatively homogeneous melts. Volatile saturation pressures of ~2.0 to 2.8 kbar, together with high vesicularities (21 to 30 vol.%) and extensive connectivities (greater than 93%), are consistent with the presence of a CO2 rich vapour phase within the mush. Nodules are erupted when a rigid crystal framework and a well connected bubble network that enables effective degassing preserve their integrity. When bubble networks fail to form, degassing disrupts the framework, even if initially rigid, causing disaggregation into plagioclase phyric crystal cargoes, akin to plagioclase ultraphyric basalts erupted in diverse settings. Nodules record the dynamics of stratified mushy magma reservoirs, the influence of volatiles on mush behaviour and the transport of crystalline material in active volcanic systems, providing key insights into how crystal mushes shape the storage and mobilisation of magma in the Earth’s crust.

MAPClean: Microstructurally Adaptive Pixel Level Cleaning

Rahul Subbaraman
A modular MATLAB pipeline built on the open source MTEX toolbox for automated pixel level restoration of large scale Electron Backscatter Diffraction (EBSD) datasets. Combines phase aware and orientation aware logic with intelligent Strict versus Relaxed protocol selection to remove noise, correct misindexed pixels and reconstruct missing data prior to grain reconstruction.
View repository and code on Zenodo (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.18052287) ↗

Overview: Specialists & General Audience

For specialists: Current EBSD workflows typically follow a grain first approach, where denoising and orientation filling depend on initial grain definition. At the mesoscale (full thin section mapping), data quality is often degraded by topographic artefacts and long acquisition times (~10 hours), leading to stage drift. This frequently prevents grain reconstruction altogether, creating a processing deadlock. MAPClean addresses this by operating at the pixel level, performing microstructurally adaptive restoration before grain reconstruction. It uses phase aware logic and parallelised cluster filling. Applied to datasets exceeding 34 million pixels across four samples, it enables recovery of crystallographic fabrics from maps previously considered unusable.

For non specialists: EBSD allows us to map how crystals are oriented inside a rock, essentially revealing its internal structure. At large scales, however, data becomes noisy due to long scan times and minor imperfections in the sample. Existing tools struggle because they require relatively clean data to begin with. MAPClean acts as a digital restoration tool, repairing noisy regions point by point using the surrounding crystal structure, allowing previously unusable datasets to be recovered.

Pipeline Architecture & Key Features
  • Modular Stage Control & Checkpointing: Enable or disable individual pipeline stages via boolean flags. Each stage saves a .mat checkpoint and exports phase and Inverse Pole Figure (IPF) maps automatically, allowing interrupted runs to resume without reprocessing.
  • Automated Protocol Selection: Automatically selects Strict mode (well indexed data) or Relaxed mode (sparse data) based on the fraction of notIndexed pixels within the physically constrained sampleMask.
  • MAD Filtering & Sample Mask Generation: Removes unreliable pixels exceeding a user defined Mean Angular Deviation threshold and estimates physically grounded specimen boundaries directly from indexed pixels.
  • Phase & Orientation Wild Spike Removal (WSR): Corrects misindexed pixels against local neighbourhoods and resolves orientation wild spikes via local misorientation clustering with twin boundary protection for Anorthite.
  • Parallelised BFS (Strict) & Iterative MPF (Relaxed) Hole Filling: Screens disconnected hole clusters for neighbourhood support and fills them in parallel via Breadth First Search, or applies vectorised Multi Pass Filling with serial dominant phase and orientation coherence checks for sparse maps.
  • Orientation Clustering & Protected Hole Filling: Assigns mean orientations via hierarchical single linkage quaternion clustering (with ring based fallback) and executes a final conservative recovery of uncertain grain interiors.

GRaMC: Grain Reconstruction and Multi stage Cleaning

Rahul Subbaraman
A heuristic grain level reconstruction and microstructural restoration framework for EBSD data designed to operate in tandem with MAPClean. Moves beyond standard MTEX reconstruction by incorporating automated phase splitting, zero GOS artefact removal, crystallographic twin merging, pixel level daughter grain reorientation and area based population filtering.
View repository and code on Zenodo (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.18107589) ↗

Overview: Specialists & General Audience

For specialists: Following pixel level restoration with MAPClean, GRaMC takes the cleaned EBSD output and applies phase aware grain reconstruction logic. Standard grain reconstruction workflows can still struggle at the mesoscale when datasets contain fragmented grains, twin related domains and artefactual grain populations. GRaMC addresses this through a multi stage reconstruction workflow incorporating adaptive cleanup, crystallographic twin merging, grain size diagnostics and population filtering. Applied downstream of MAPClean, it enables the generation of physically consistent, analysis ready grain datasets from large and previously problematic EBSD maps.

For non specialists: After noisy EBSD maps are restored, the next challenge is deciding which mapped points belong to the same crystal. This is not always straightforward because crystals may be broken into fragments by noise, polishing effects or internal structures such as twins. GRaMC acts as a digital grain reconstruction tool. It rebuilds crystals from cleaned EBSD data, removes unreliable grain artefacts, merges related crystal fragments and produces grain maps that can be used for later microstructural and texture analysis.

Pipeline Architecture & Key Features
  • Phase Splitting & Multi stage Checkpointing: Automatically splits MAPClean outputs into phase specific .ctf files (processing Diopside, Forsterite and Anorthite independently) with checkpointed inspection at every major reconstruction stage.
  • Initial Reconstruction & GOS = 0 Cleanup: Reconstructs grains using a fixed misorientation threshold, removes sub resolution noise below a minimum pixel count and resets artefactual zero Grain Orientation Spread (GOS = 0) grains in Anorthite to notIndexed prior to recalculation.
  • Crystallographic Twin Boundary Merging: Automatically detects and merges Anorthite grains separated by specific twin laws: Albite, Pericline, Carlsbad, Manebach, Baveno Right, Baveno Left, Ala_A and Ala_B.
  • Pixel Level Daughter Reorientation & Property Recalculation: Rotates daughter grain EBSD pixels into the parent reference frame after twin merging to maintain physical consistency and recalculates area weighted mean orientations and GOS.
  • Grain Size Diagnostics & Population Filtering: Exports log10(area) histograms and colour coded grain maps to apply sample specific area thresholds, isolating target crystal frameworks from fine grained microlite populations.

GRaFT: Grain Resolved analysis of Fabric and Texture

Rahul Subbaraman
A dual environment analytical workflow bridging MATLAB and Python for process agnostic quantitative texture and microstructural analysis of reconstructed EBSD datasets. While GRaMC outputs can be fed into any custom statistical script, GRaFT was built using identical variable naming conventions and data structures to provide a seamless, end to end pipeline from raw EBSD data to statistical synthesis.
View repository and code on Zenodo (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.18107626) ↗

Overview: Specialists & General Audience

For specialists: Although the reconstructed grain outputs from GRaMC (*_finalGrains.mat) can be used as input for any custom statistical or analytical code, GRaFT was developed as the dedicated third stage of the MAPClean ecosystem. By maintaining identical variable naming conventions and data structures across all three tools, it establishes a seamless workflow from raw pixel level EBSD data to final statistical synthesis. The workflow separates computation across two linked environments: the MATLAB and MTEX stage processes *_finalGrains.mat objects to compute Orientation Distribution Functions (ODFs), fabric tensors, grain scale morphological parameters, pixel scale intracrystalline deformation metrics (GOS, GROD and KAM) and grain contact pair geometries, exporting 11 publication ready map types per phase. A companion Python workflow ingests the exported CSV datasets for distributional analysis, population comparison and cross sample synthesis.

For non specialists: Once individual crystals have been digitally rebuilt from a rock scan by GRaMC, that data is ready to be analysed by any statistical software. GRaFT was built as a dedicated companion tool to make the transition from raw scan data to final statistics completely automatic. It measures the shapes, sizes, alignments and internal strain of the reconstructed crystals, generates visual maps of their properties and transfers the results directly into Python to compare crystal populations across different rock samples.

Pipeline Architecture & Key Features
  • Seamless End to End Ecosystem: While GRaMC outputs (*_finalGrains.mat) are compatible with any external analysis script, GRaFT shares exact variable naming conventions and data structures with MAPClean and GRaMC to provide a unified, three part pipeline from raw EBSD data to statistical interpretation.
  • Dual Environment Architecture: Separates crystallographic and geometric computation in MATLAB and MTEX (EBSD loading, grain object analysis, checkpointing, map export and CSV generation) from downstream statistical plotting and cross sample synthesis in Python.
  • Core Texture & Fabric Quantification: Automates Orientation Distribution Function (ODF) calculation, Pole Figure (PF) and Inverse Pole Figure (IPF) generation, texture strength quantification and fabric tensor analysis across Anorthite, Forsterite and Diopside.
  • Grain Scale Morphological Analysis: Quantifies Equivalent Circular Diameter (ECD), aspect ratio, long axis orientation, ellipse fitting and crystal shape visualisations for every reconstructed grain.
  • Pixel Scale Intracrystalline Deformation: Maps internal crystal strain using Grain Orientation Spread (GOS), Grain Reference Orientation Deviation (GROD), Kernel Average Misorientation (KAM) and GROD/GOS ratio distributions.
  • Contact Network & Pair Analysis: Evaluates local framework organisation by computing grain pair misorientations alongside long axis and crystallographic axis angular comparisons.
  • Automated 11 Map Export Suite: Exports 11 standardised publication ready maps per mineral phase: 01 Pole Figure (PF), 02 Inverse Pole Figure (IPF), 03 Aspect Ratio Map, 04 Grain Size (ECD) Map, 05 Long Axis Orientation Map, 06 GOS Map, 07 GROD Map, 08 GROD/GOS Ratio Map, 09 Crystal Shape Map, 10 KAM Map and 11 Ellipse Fit Map.

DenCalc: Density Calculator

Rahul Subbaraman
A Python based thermodynamic modelling utility utilising pandas and NumPy to compute phase specific and bulk densities of Icelandic gabbroic nodules and their carrier melts. Integrates multicomponent oxide compositions, pressure and temperature estimates and modal proportions to evaluate carrier melt, interstitial melt and crystalline phase densities.
View repository and code on Zenodo (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.16943747) ↗

Overview: Specialists & General Audience

For specialists: DenCalc is a Python based thermodynamic calculation workflow designed to quantify the buoyancy and physical properties of Icelandic gabbroic nodules and their host magmas. The pipeline ingests average phase oxide compositions (Average_Phase_Comp.csv), thermobarometric constraints (OPAM_HS24_PT.csv) and modal phase proportions (Modal_Propn.csv) to compute densities across three distinct structural domains: carrier melt (scoria matrix glass), interstitial melt (nodule matrix) and mineral phases (plagioclase, olivine and clinopyroxene). Liquid densities incorporate empirical dissolved water estimations (H2O = MgO * 0.052) alongside thermodynamic pressure and temperature corrections, before combining phase specific densities with modal proportions to output bulk nodule density records (exports/Density.csv).

For non specialists: Whether crystal clusters float, sink or get carried to the surface during a volcanic eruption depends on their density compared to the surrounding liquid magma. DenCalc is a Python calculator that determines the exact density of both the solid crystals and the molten rock at deep underground pressures and temperatures. By combining the chemical makeup of each mineral with its volume fraction inside the rock, the tool calculates the overall density of the crystal nodules and the host magma.

Pipeline Architecture & Key Features
  • Automated Geochemical & Thermobarometric Ingestion: Reads Average_Phase_Comp.csv to partition compositional dataframes into carrier melt (liq), interstitial matrix melt (mat) and crystalline phases (xl), dynamically appending pressure and temperature constraints from OPAM_HS24_PT.csv.
  • Liquid & Matrix Melt Density Calculation: Passes liq and mat structures through calculate_liquid_density(), applying thermodynamic corrections and empirical dissolved water scaling (H2O = MgO * 0.052).
  • Crystalline Phase Density Modelling: Evaluates solid phase densities across plagioclase, olivine and clinopyroxene by passing xl compositions and pressure and temperature conditions through calculate_mineral_density().
  • Modal Integration & Bulk Nodule Synthesis: Combines phase resolved density dataframes with modal mineralogy (propn from Modal_Propn.csv) via compute_bulk_density(), exporting unified liquid, matrix, mineral and bulk nodule density tables to exports/Density.csv.

MOSAIC: Multivariate Compositional Framework

Rahul Subbaraman
A comprehensive open source statistical framework for the multivariate analysis of compositional datasets. Integrates unsupervised machine learning (PCA, k means), robust distributional comparisons (Wasserstein distances, bootstrap resampling) and multidimensional variance metrics (Mahalanobis distances) to quantify latent population structures and complex chemical heterogeneity.

Rahul Subbaraman
Department of Earth and Environmental Sciences, University of Manchester
Date made available: 2 Apr 2026 • Publisher: University of Manchester Figshare
Access dataset on Figshare (DOI: 10.48420/31914846) ↗

Description
This dataset contains the processing outputs generated by MAPClean, a MATLAB-based EBSD map cleaning and processing tool, applied to electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) data from gabbroic nodules collected at Gígöldur, Iceland. Samples contain plagioclase, clinopyroxene, and olivine. The dataset is organised into three components: (1) figure outputs, arranged by sample name, including inverse pole figure (IPF) maps for all phases present and phase maps generated at each successive stage of the cleaning workflow, allowing direct visual comparison of map quality before and after each processing step; (2) log files recording processing parameters and decisions for each sample; and (3) checkpoint files enabling reproduction or resumption of processing runs. These outputs are released to support replication of results reported in an associated forthcoming publication, and to enable community reuse of MAPClean with reference material from a natural petrological dataset.

Description

Thin section scans of gabbro nodules, scoria, plagioclase-phyric basalt, hyaloclastite from Gígöldur, central Iceland

Rahul Subbaraman, Margaret Hartley and David Neave
Department of Earth and Environmental Sciences, The University of Manchester
Date made available: 10 Mar 2025 • Publisher: University of Manchester Figshare
Access dataset on Figshare (DOI: 10.48420/28435859.v1) ↗

Description
Plane-polarised light (PPL), partial cross-polarised light (PXPL), and cross-polarised light (XPL) thin section scans were obtained for gabbroic nodules, scoria, plagioclase-phyric basalt, and hyaloclastite from Gígöldur, central Iceland. The PXPL scans are captured with a polariser-analyser angle of 45° to avoid the complete extinction of the silicate phases. The scale bars represent 5mm. Gígöldur is a broad NNE–SSW-trending ridge composed of interlayered hyaloclastites and postglacial craters, located in the highlands north of Vatnajökull glacier. Samples were collected from three locations along the ridge: Gígöldur North (GO23-04), Gígöldur Central (GO19-01 & GO23-01), and Gígöldur South (GO19-02, GO23-02 & GO23-03). The nodules, 5-10 cm in size, are rounded to sub-angular and consist primarily of plagioclase aggregates encased in glass. They contain small but variable amounts of interstitial olivine and, occasionally, dark green clinopyroxene. The scoria clasts, 2-5 cm in size, are highly vesicular, with predominantly circular, uncoalesced vesicles. The hyaloclastite samples consist of angular glass shards, plagioclase and olivine crystals, and fine-grained volcanic debris. The plagioclase-phyric basalt consists of plagioclase and mafic phenocrysts set in a variably porous glassy matrix.

Abstract
This dataset comprises 3D X ray computed tomography (XCT) scans of eight rock cores (15 mm diameter) prepared from two gabbroic nodules collected at Gígöldur, Central Iceland.
